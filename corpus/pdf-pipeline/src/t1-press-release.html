Lattice Harbor Raises $48M Series B to Expand Its Supply-Chain Data Clean Room Platform

Round led by Meridian Crest Capital brings total funding to $71M; company will open a European region and double its applied-research team by year end.

PORT ELLISON, Ore. — February 24, 2026 — Lattice Harbor, the data collaboration platform that lets manufacturers and their suppliers analyze shared logistics data without exposing proprietary records, today announced a $48 million Series B financing led by Meridian Crest Capital, with participation from existing investors Quayside Ventures and Fjordlight Partners.

The company's clean-room technology allows two or more supply-chain partners to run joint forecasting and capacity models against encrypted datasets that never leave each party's infrastructure. Since launching its general-availability product eighteen months ago, Lattice Harbor has grown to 140 enterprise customers across automotive, consumer electronics, and cold-chain pharmaceutical logistics, and now processes collaborative queries over more than nine petabytes of partner data.

"Supply chains run on information their participants are rightly unwilling to hand over," said Priya Venkataraman, co-founder and chief executive officer. "Our customers have shown that you can get the forecasting benefits of pooled data without anyone surrendering a single row of it. This round lets us bring that model to the European manufacturers who have been asking for a local region since the day we launched."

The new capital will fund a Frankfurt-hosted deployment region scheduled for the third quarter, expanded ERP and WMS connector coverage, and growth of the applied-research group. Rafael Osei, general partner at Meridian Crest Capital, will join the company's board of directors.

About Lattice Harbor

Founded in 2022, Lattice Harbor builds privacy-preserving data collaboration infrastructure for industrial supply chains. The company is headquartered in Port Ellison, Oregon, with offices in Toronto and Singapore, and employs 180 people across nine countries.
