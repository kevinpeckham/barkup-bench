Investment Thesis

Stonebridge Growth Partners invests in family-owned precision manufacturers at the moment of generational transition. Across the upper Midwest, an estimated 3,100 machine shops, toolmakers, and specialty fabricators are led by owners over the age of sixty, most without an identified successor. These are profitable, quietly indispensable businesses — suppliers of record to aerospace, medical-device, and grid-equipment programs — whose order books routinely extend further than their ownership plans.

Fund III continues the strategy of its predecessors: acquire controlling interests in eight to eleven such companies, retain and incentivize the operating teams, and invest in the automation and certifications that unlock the next tier of contracts. We do not relocate plants, we do not pursue roll-up synergies at the cost of customer relationships, and we have never re-sold a portfolio company to a competitor of its own customers.

Funds I and II acquired fifteen companies on this model. Thirteen remain under Stonebridge ownership today; aggregate revenue across the portfolio has grown 9.4 percent annually since acquisition, and every plant operates in its original county. Two companies have been sold — both to their own management teams, financed in part by the fund, which we consider the thesis working as designed.

Fund III targets commitments of $240 million, with a hard cap of $275 million. The general partners commit a minimum of four percent of the fund's total. Detailed terms, risk factors, and audited track-record schedules appear in the full offering memorandum, which this summary does not replace.
